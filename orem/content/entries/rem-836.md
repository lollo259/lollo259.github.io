## From the source: Fairy Tales
<!-- type: description | source: Fairy Tales | translated: no -->
In the shadow of the great wooden wall, whispers grew of the "immortal swordsman" whose body could not be claimed by death. The Daimyo, a man of wealth and power, had grown increasingly paranoid following the construction of the barrier. His nights were plagued by the specter of a love lost and a vendetta unfulfilled. His wife, the former geisha known as Komurasaki, had become a silent sentinel in their opulent chamber, her eyes haunted by the memories of a man she could never truly claim as her own. The wall, a monument to fear, stretched for miles, a stark reminder of the divide that separated the living from the damned. Yet, within the sanctity of their home, she had become a prisoner of her own making, her thoughts forever entangled with the man she had tried to save. Each day, she would stare out the window, her gaze searching for the ghost of Zoro, her heart a cage of sorrow. The rumors of his undying nature had reached her ears, a cruel twist of fate that mocked her own inability to escape the chains of life. And though she knew their love was as cold as the stone that now entombed his heart, she could not help but feel a pang of guilt for the curse she had bestowed upon him. Yet, in the quiet of her soul, she harbored a flicker of hope that one day, somehow, she would find a way to free them both from the prison of immortality.

In the grand hall of the Flower Capital, the melody of a shamisen echoed through the rafters, a haunting tune that seemed to carry the weight of unshed tears. On the dais, the beautiful and enigmatic geisha known as Komurasaki played with a fierce intensity, her eyes hidden behind the mask of the kitsune. Her fingers danced over the strings, a silent rebellion against the words that had been flung at the warrior she loved. As she played, the string snapped with a sound that pierced the air like a cry of despair. The Daimyo's eyes narrowed, his voice a cold whisper of displeasure. "Your incompetence is an affront to my hospitality," he sneered, his words a knife twisting in her heart. She knew the punishment that awaited her - a dance of pain and humiliation that she had endured countless times before. Yet, as she left the room, the echoes of her song trailing after her, she felt a newfound strength. For she had felt the warmth of Zoro's presence, a phantom touch that reminded her of the love that burned within her. And in that moment, she vowed that she would not be broken, not even by the most powerful man in Wano. Her spirit, like the string she had played, might bend, but it would never truly break.

The once-magnificent hall of the king now bore the taint of a dark secret. As the lord of the land grew more and more obsessed with the idea of immortality, he had turned his gaze upon the very essence of life itself. His eyes, once filled with the warmth of humanity, had turned cold and calculating, seeing the natural world as a mere tool to be manipulated and consumed. His banquets, once a celebration of bounty and kinship, had transformed into macabre feasts where the flesh of his enemies was served alongside the slain beasts of the land. Yet, the most terrible revelation was the fate of his own beloved wife, whose body had become the very symbol of his twisted desire. Whenever the aroma of freshly killed game wafted through the air, the daimyo would cut a piece from her, a grisly mimicry of nature's cycle. Her pained cries echoed through the corridors, a haunting melody that soon became a part of the castle's very foundation. The animals of the kingdom, once revered as kin, now feared the castle's shadow, knowing the fate that awaited them within its walls.
