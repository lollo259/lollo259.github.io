Orpheus performs the black mass as a priest consumes the body of Christ. This analogy is not rhetoric but structural: the Christian mass is transubstantiation — the bread becomes flesh, the wine becomes blood, and the faithful literally incorporates the deity. Similarly, black mass is a rite of ontological transformation: being becomes non-being, presence becomes absence, memory becomes oblivion.

♪ Black Mass on Himself: Cicisbeo as Auto-Cancellation

Has Orpheus ever considered performing black Mass on himself? In a way, he already did.

The act of the cicisbeus — the body of Orpheus which lets itself be corrupted by the body of Ishtar, which grants its own meat without resistance, which is offered as an instrument — is a form of black mass. Not identical to the formal ceremony with Aphrodite, but structurally analogous.

In classical black mass, the god gives body, mind, heart, and then fades. The cicisbeus gave the body (completely possessed by Ishtar), gave the mind (it has no more own thoughts, it is pure instinct), gave the heart (it does not feel emotions, does not love, does not want consciously).

What about Orpheus' soul? It remains apathetic, devoid of will and will own. It is forgotten by itself*. He does not recognize the cicisbeus as part of himself. He doesn't remember being one. The soul has performed the black mass on the body, and the result is this permanent split.

But if the logic of the black mass implies that what is deleted *return* as potential... where is the body-like-potential of Orpheus? Is it in the Nightmare Morningstar? Is it in the corrupt flesh of the cicisbeus that, although dead, continues to exist?
