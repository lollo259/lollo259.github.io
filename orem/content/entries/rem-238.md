## Mentions in other documents: Central Mind In The Dark
<!-- type: description | source: Central Mind In The Dark | translated: no -->
It was always there. My voyage started in a place I couldn’t dream of. I had to write it down. The Land That Tells No Tales, I called it. It was a cold desert made out of graphite and dust, and clouds made of smoke.  Creatures built with sails and tubes wandered around, and only via close inspection did something become clear: they were not alive, merely mechanisms that moved with the wind. My drafts, perhaps. What I had left in my mind. I decided to walk for a little while. After all, what else could I do? The more time I spent on that landscape, the more I remembered all my archived dreams, and my hopes. The sound of the gentle wind resembled a pencil on a piece of paper. What an oddity. Of all the things I could find in that place, never had I ever imagined coming across a colossal monument valley. Not one made by nature, no. It was manmade. It was a city of geometrical, minimal blocks of cement, with its roads excavated in the ground. The distant mountains I used to see until then were no longer visible, as I was standing below ground level. Three, colossal dials were placed one near the other, except there were no numbers and no proper indication of their function. In addition, they were casting shadows following the light from above the clouds, but I couldn’t manage to properly see what was the source. I felt incredibly lonely, being in a place with sculptures, but no sculptors. Was I the only one there? What makes me different from what I see? Am I alive? The short answer was no. The long answer was still no. I couldn’t explain any better what I was thinking. I couldn’t feel the distance between my body and that landscape. There were no boundaries. I couldn’t remember anything about my life, and all the experiences that led to the creation of my ego. While it was dead, my body simply responded mechanically, or chemically, or however it did, to that silent place. I couldn’t think about myself. There was no self and no reason for it to be. I wanted to cry, and when I did, my ego resurrected. Suddenly blood was raining all over the place. The landscape was breathing, and their breathing was made out of ashes. As foggy clouds fell down surrounding the area, I reached for the entrance of the city, hoping to escape the incoming horrors. As blood was flooding the valley, I fainted in panic, and everything went black. What happened, I later realized, was the landscape rejecting my ego, for the place that tells no tales has no ego. I asked myself if it meant imprisonment, as for as long as I kept my ego exiled, there was an endless existence in a limbo between alive and dead. Would I feel anything, at all? Maybe not, but at least I was able to see. Not blinded by the melancholy of that place, I pursued my journey. I was merely guided by instinct, to not anger my surroundings. While passing on a field of steel pillars, exhausted by the long voyage, thunderstorms began to light up the place, and there I noticed the distant mountains were in fact colossal sandstorms. The terrain felt like quicksand and slowly morphed into muddy water. Again I fainted. Again I woke up. Nothing had changed, the landscape was the same desert as before. And then I saw it. I saw where the light that was illuminating the place came from. There was a grand palace based on the surmounting clouds, but I couldn’t see where the support of the structure was. It was not standing anywhere. The Church Of Light was casting shadows of brightness on the entire landscape, illuminating a place without any witnesses. Surely a strange sight for the gaze. The entrance was on the horizon, a vulva made out of petals flowed on each other. As I was acting only via instincts alone, that universal symbol was forcing all my urges. It was the entrance through which every human being is born, the same place where they exploit their deepest desires, and metaphorically where they return to die. As I started walking towards that symbol for what seemed like an eternity, the shape of the palace started to bend, almost as if I was reaching the event horizon of a black hole. At one point it was too large to be even seen, and that’s when I walked through the entrance. From the inside, the walls were like distant boundaries, seemingly non-existent. Only at that moment did I notice that the floor was a pile of bodies, titanic deities gliding on each other slowly moving in unison, like worms. It was none other , a cluster of deities enduring in physical orgies. A war waging for eons, no one destined for victory but an infinite surface of harmony. The bodies covered in sweat glowed in an alluring warmth, smelling like flowers, and the steam created a light mist, rendering the place less luminous. It is said that if you lick their skin nothing will ever be as tasty. Ejaculating in ecstasy, juices were overflowing creating small cascades, rivers, and pools of lust, passion and bliss. The silhouettes of the bodies were like magnets to each other, clinging and sticking to the soft skin, and wet marks were present even in the driest parts. I stumbled on that mess, and my hand acted as a third leg for better balance. When I looked down, a body giggled after my inadvertent cuddle. When I proceeded to walk, a spurt of squirt fountained on my face. That's when I decided to stop venturing, choosing to look around. On my left, probably 30 meters from me, the goddess Aphrodite [[REM-110](#/rem-110)] was resurfacing from the Peerage, her feet stomping on the bodies below. Once she stood up, there was another figure in front of her, someone I thought I would never meet in person.
