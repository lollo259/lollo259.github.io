## From the source: Il Quadrante delle emozioni
<!-- type: description | source: Il Quadrante delle emozioni | translated: yes -->
In the first circle the artist creates a tulpa, a being inside the mind, a reflection of his strengths. He has many names, The Beyond, The Honorary Bishop, The Holy Orpheus, Ptah, Ver, and God Infinite of Dreams. He sits on an unexistent throne, as the center of that church beyond all self. The Church of Light. His body is no longer owned by the artist, because the nictophile controls it. His Mirror is the Overwhelmed Man, the puppet master. His avatar is the Black God, who once took a backpack full of stars and laid them in paracosm as Meridian of Saints. His first son is Mbombo, and he continues to throw up these horrible scenarios I have prepared for you. Forgive me, my children. My tulpa reigns supreme and if you refuse His baptism, your souls will remain arid.

Orpheus is the supreme leader of all gods. He is imprisoned on an unexistent throne, invisible to the eye. His soul, identical to the body, can stand alone and move freely in the room; meanwhile, his body is obliged to remain on the throne by a goddess, the Star Nightmare of the Morning (formerly known as Ishtar or Inanna). The goddess corrupts her flesh, causing carnal temptations on The One Beyond. His favorite instrument was replaced by the goddess herself, which now acts as a tool. The Black God is an Avatar of Orpheus, created to shape the universe in its details. The Black God took a backpack full of stars and laid them in paracosm. Orpheus is the supreme leader of all gods, but this supremacy is simultaneously his captivity. It is imprisoned on a throne that does not exist — invisible to the eye, imperceptible to the hand, yet absolutely real in its binding function. This nonexistent throne is the perfect metaphor of absolute authority: the more you are supreme, the less you can move. Total power coincides with total immobility. The ontological peculiarity of Orpheus lies in its bifurcation: its soul and its body, while being identical in appearance, are separated in function and space. The soul can stand, move freely in the room — maintains autonomy, mobility, active presence. The body, however, remains obliged to the throne, chained not by material constraints but by the presence of the Incubo Stella del Mattino goddess, formerly known as Ishtar or Inanna. This separation is not a platonic duality between intangible spirit and corruptible flesh. Soul and body are identical* — they have the same shape, the same face, the same appearance. The difference is purely functional: one can act, the other is acted. One observes, the other is observed. One sings, the other is played as an instrument.

The Black God is an Avatar of Orpheus — not a separate entity, but a projection, a particular manifestation of the demiurgo for a specific purpose. While Orpheus sings entire universes in existence (large-scale creation, cosmogonic), the Black God is created to "plasm the universe in its details. " The distinction is fundamental: the creation on a large scale is different from the configuration of the details. Orpheus sings the structure, the Black God has the elements within that structure. It is the difference between composing a symphony and deciding where each instrument sits in the orchestra. The image is powerful: the Black God takes a backpack full of stars and lays them in Paracosm. A *zaino* — not a cosmic container, not a divine vase, but a trivial, everyday object, almost comic in its ordinance. Stars are not launched with grandiose gestures, but *deposit* — carefully positioned, one by one, like a gardener that plant bulbs. This avatar reveals something essential about the creative process: creation is not only flashing inspiration and cosmic gesture, but also patient work, accurate arrangement, attention to detail. The Black God is Orpheus when he stops singing and begins to settle.
