## Description
<!-- type: article -->
The Orem is mostly the afterlife: the realm and the organization (the "Organization" of the dossiers) where the dead go. The sources call it humanity's only hope. It stands on Paradis, the planet and goddess of the first book. Its name is also read as "theorem": an organization of the lower creatures, created to unite all creatures and defeat the gods.

## Purpose and method
<!-- type: article -->
A single creature is nothing next to a god, and even an anthill of creatures is small next to one. The Orem is not a simple anthill: its units attack the body of the gods like a virus. It studies the structure of the divine, the "pastures in heaven", and codifies its parts in order to bring it to collapse. When the Creation is at war with its Creator, the mind collapses and the Artist no longer rules the domain.

## Structure
<!-- type: article -->
The Machinist [REM-XXX](#/rem-xxx), its founder, took the Tecnica and divided it among the members (five, or however many the Orem has), giving each a specialty and keeping for himself the ability to create artifacts.

Its zones of interest include Babel, a branch dedicated to the writing of books; the Library [REM-126](#/rem-126) with the Bestiary; and The Farm [REM-371](#/rem-371), structures in the asteroid ring "Armageddon" run by the Astral Task Force for experimentation and for the re-education and training of new recruits, the dead. Its elite units are the Terra Task Forces [REM-319](#/rem-319), the "best of the best" of the Organization.

## Conflict with the Saints
<!-- type: article -->
In the saga plan the fourth book is "the classic good vs bad, Orem vs Saints". The Orem keeps technical observations on the council of the gods (the Wargods, the Horsemen and the judge Hecatekali [REM-148](#/rem-148)), and the events of [REM-216](#/rem-216) are "the final battle of the wars between Saints and Orem".

The river people hunt octopuses with the traditional Japanese octopus pot (takotsubo). They do not kill the octopus: it is freed after giving its ink.

Water recycling: filters that spin 24/7. They receive the sewer water, and a slowly rotating arm spreads it over a filter bed (as in a trickling filter). The water is souls; outside the Geraas Walls it becomes blood. The dirt goes down into the sewers, into the lower dimension.

The river people farm the way rice is grown together with fish (rice–fish farming, carp in the paddies); here the fish are shinigami.

## Author's notes: the bookbinders (16 Oct 2026)
<!-- type: note | source: Author's chat notes (16 Oct 2026) -->
The prairie and river people bind pages into books for the ever-expanding Library [REM-126](#/rem-126). The Prairies map places the Library under the desert of Falari [REM-108](#/rem-108), below the dam. They cut trees for the pages. The ink is the souls (the water) mixed with octopus ink; it is what they write with. They write in Assamic script, the language of Orpheus [REM-000](#/rem-000). They sew the pages with needles. The covers are wood bark. How they are pressed is still open. The stories are told to them by the sky people, the most transcendent, nearest to the Church of Light [REM-123](#/rem-123).

## Author's notes: bone-threaded gods and the afterlife of the gods (16 Oct 2026)
<!-- type: note | source: Author's chat notes (16 Oct 2026) -->
Crossing the Geraas Walls [REM-120](#/rem-120) is the afterlife of the gods: whoever goes there is forgotten. The Watchers outside the Walls (the Engineers) are technically the wall people of Innominandum [REM-168](#/rem-168). They go outside, find pieces (bones and other remains) and stitch or knot them together with threads, creating new gods: new Watchers, new Engineers. They never wander too far, or they would reach the full desert and encounter Falari [REM-108](#/rem-108). They do not know what would happen to them there; they would be completely forgotten.

## Author's notes: the micro-sculptor (16 Oct 2026)
<!-- type: note | source: Author's chat notes (16 Oct 2026) -->
A new character, not yet named, clearly based on the real-life micro-sculptor Willard Wigan. He lives somewhere in the Skies. He makes microscopic sculptures, small enough to fit in a needle, and drops them to the sewers, where they are normal size; perhaps as a way of taunting them.
