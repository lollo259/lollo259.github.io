## From the source: Paradis i Ander Reus [9/4/23]
<!-- type: description | source: Paradis i Ander Reus [9/4/23] | translated: no -->
Their primary prey is the titan kalamar, who lives in the twilight zone. It's 20 meters long and its left eye is at least twice the diameter of the right eye, tubular, yellow-green, faces upward, and bulges out of the head, making it able to see ambient sunlight and li'atans from the euphotic zone, and its smaller eye is able to detect bioluminescence from prey in the darkness. Their favorite prey is the si dooragon, a family of two species of ferocious fish. They also have giant hooks on their tentacles and arms, to catch prey and defend themselves against their predator. When they spot a li'atan they usually attack them. Their strength is incredible, and the battles between kalamars and li'atans are a sight to be seen.

Li'atans have domesticated the lisodålphin (let. "lissodolphin"), just for mere fun, in a period of approximately 300 years.

Li'atans sometimes sacrifice themselves to fulfill the "greater good of the seas", leaving their carcasses to create whale fall ecosystems.

The si dooragon is a family of deep sea fish that doesn't reach more than 1 meter in length. These fish are all apex predators, in an environment where they are each other's prey. They have fang-like teeth, and enormous jaws.

The loosejau (318 cm) has a rattrap-like mouth almost detached to its body.

The sualouer (190 cm) is much smaller, but has an expandable stomach and is able to eat prey as much as twice its size. The sualouer attacks the loosejaw from its tail, avoiding its jaw, while on the other end the loosejau attacks from the front, snapping its jaw to incapacitate their adversary.

The telescope fish (180 cm) has two enormous eyes that looks like binoculars.

In the open seas giant snails swim. In their larval stage, the so called larvacean swim inside a mucus structure made by themselves that acts like a house. When they reach adulthood their mucus is way stronger and denser than before, and it's therefore able to make shells. They have different adult forms:

larvacean turbelaria (turbellarians) are the most common, as they don't have a shell and therefore they need to have large numbers.

larvacean carinaria (carinaria lamarcki) have a tiny shell where they cannot retract, and a huge body;

larvacean pharfalea (sea butterflies) have spiral shells and two feet that act like fins. They swim upside down.

larvacean limacea (sea snails) have spiral shells and live suspended on the surface of the sea attached to bubbles.

larvacean conea (cone snails) have a protruding dart used to impale and poison their prey.
