Laurence Who (Lawrence Hu), multidisciplinary creative producer

Laurence Who (Lorenzo Ritacco) is a multidisciplinary creative producer, composer and pianist creating projects that join music, art, film, magic and design.

who i am

Laurence Who is an artist, author of Asombros (a concerto for magician and ensemble, created with illusionists Diego Allegri and Nicolò Tosin) and of the saga The Great Silence (created with more than fifty artists). He writes polytonal chamber music, post-minimalist operas, songs, hip-hop and lo-fi productions, electroacoustic music, improvisations and hybrid compositions. He graduated with the highest marks and honours in Piano and Chamber Music. His signature piece is George Gershwin’s Rhapsody in Blue.

Laurence Who is an artist, author of Asombros (a concerto for magician and ensemble, created in collaboration with illusionists Diego Allegri and Nicolò Tosin) and of the saga The Great Silence (created in collaboration with more than fifty artists, including multimedia artist Andrew Quinn, architect Alfonso Firmani, sculptor Calogero Condello, and many others). He has written music for Luca Trabucco, Stefano Sciascia, Duo LaRoi, Duo En Blanc Et Noir, Trio Ives, Duo Morpheus, and many others, including the short films Crisalide and Invisibili by Valentina Ercolini. He writes polytonal chamber music, post-minimalist operas, songs, hip-hop and lo-fi productions, electroacoustic music, improvisations, hybrid compositions, and conceptual art exercises. In the past, he wrote news reports and in-depth articles about professional wrestling. He graduated with the highest marks, honours, and an honorary mention in both Piano and Chamber Music, and was recognised among the Young People of Distinction of the Municipality of Udine. He mainly performs “free” music (American music and niche repertoire), using in particular the sculpture Marilyn from the catalogue of sculptor Franco Maschio to perform Charles Ives’s Concord Sonata and his own trio piece Somewhere In My Heart, although his signature piece is George Gershwin’s Rhapsody in Blue. A Life

2006
Begins piano lessons with Federica Repini at Fondazione Bon.
2011
Enters the Conservatorio di Udine.
2012
Continues studies with Fabiana Barbini.
2013
Continues studies with Massimiliano Ferrati.
2015
Enrolls at the Liceo Artistico “G. Sello.”
2016
Starts watching professional wrestling, soon writing news coverage and features on it.
2017
Completes core studies at Udine; begins the triennio there with Luca Trabucco; begins the Architettura e ambiente section of the liceo.
2018
Named among the Giovani di Pregio of the Comune di Udine; begins the first drafts of what will become The Great Silence.
2019
Starts writing what will become Asombros.
2020
Finishes high school with a thesis/final project titled T.O.E. Museum (Theory of Everything Museum); leaves the triennio at Udine in its final year to follow Trabucco to the Conservatorio di Trieste.
2021
Finishes the triennio in Trieste with top marks, with a graduation program built entirely around Gershwin — two waltzes in C, Rhapsody in Blue, and more.
2022
Enrolls in the Chamber Music degree track at the Conservatorio di Trieste.
2023
Writes the first actual chapter of The Great Silence. Develops De Quervain tendonitis in his right hand. Sits two exams and graduates the Biennio in Pianoforte on 13 July, with top marks and honorable mention, despite the injury. Starts a contemporary-piano course in Pinerolo with Emanuele Arciuli in the second half of the year.
2024
Wins first prize and the critics’ prize at Premio Argento and the Premio Lilian Caraian with Duo En Blanc Et Noir. Debuts with his trio at the Auditorium Rai FVG in Trieste on 11 September — and the same tendonitis flares up again the very next morning. Plays a second concert with the trio on 14 September, then stops playing entirely and steps away from the conservatorio for about a year.
2025
Wins the absolute platinum prize at the WTPA Finland competition with Duo En Blanc Et Noir. Publishes his first book, Children of Silence, in August. Conceives the Laurence Who identity in the second half of the year.
2026
Graduates the Chamber Music degree in March, with top marks and honorable mention. Formally establishes the Laurence Who identity, and brings together a wide cluster of long-developing projects — concert works, chamber collaborations, an art book, and a live experimental film — consolidating years of separate threads into a single active body of work.
